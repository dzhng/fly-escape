It replaces the accumulated per-pass ledger: superseded build-order rows are folded into the decision that survives, reverted choices are gone, and "gate X passed on run Y" narration is dropped.

Each entry says whether it is a **user decision** (listed so the consequence is visible, not to re-open it) or an **implementation decision** (filled in where the spec was silent). Tuning numbers are named provisional where they are; none of the placement, timing or threshold numbers rest on held-out validation.

## Provisional — accepted for MVP, least certain first

### N1. Escapes produced by authored suction count the same as escapes the player earned
**Implementation decision, inside a user-requested mechanic.**

A fly wanders into the exit room. From then on, as long as it has an unobstructed line to the window opening, the world adds a steady velocity of 0.06 world units per second toward a point just outside the opening — everywhere in that room — rising to 0.5 within 0.9 m of the opening, the two combined by taking the larger (`crates/sim/src/body.rs`; authored per level as `exitSuction` in `apps/web/src/levels/open-window.ts` and `turn-the-corner.ts`). It is a physical force on the body, not a signal to the brain: it respects walls, line of sight and normal swept-collision/crossing checks, and escape still counts only on a swept outward crossing. You asked for exactly this assistance.

A no-added-object control can earn the first star: the final campaign produced 2 escapes in house 1 and 6 in house 2, against a first threshold of 1. This is a furnished house, not an empty environment. It shows that earning a star need not demonstrate successful placement for every seed; it does not establish a free unlock for every player. Suction and thresholds were chosen separately, and the user explicitly retained both.

- **Provisional call:** keep physical assistance and count every valid escape. Compare placements against the same no-added-object control when doing future balance work.
- **Confidence:** low in calibrated difficulty; high in the intended physical crossing rule. No threshold change is authorized by this ledger.

### N2. Stars two and three stay at 8 and 15 escapes of 20
**User decision, recorded for its consequence.**

The thresholds remain 1/8/15 in both authored levels. An earlier reduction was reverted at the user's instruction. No held-out player study establishes attainability or the intended roughly ten-percent three-star rate; the small observed samples cannot justify predicting what most players will achieve.

- **Provisional call:** retain the user-selected thresholds and defer difficulty calibration.
- **Confidence:** low in star-frequency predictions, high that the shipped values match the user's decision.

### N3. Odor current is graded by concentration, using a constant fitted to one house
**Implementation decision inside a user-authorized exploration.**

Before: any odor above the detection floor sent the same fixed current to the stronger antenna's population — a faint plume across the house and a banana underfoot were identical inputs. Now the winning side's current is scaled by `c / (c + 0.55)`: stronger smell, stronger current, with diminishing returns and a hard ceiling at the pathway gain (`crates/sim/src/sensory.rs`). Vision deliberately stays all-or-nothing — brightness picks a side and nothing more. Tests were rewritten to pin properties (rises with concentration, mirror-symmetric, bounded by gain, motor readouts excluded) rather than the old fixed amplitudes (`sensory.rs`; `crates/sim/tests/attempt.rs` now composes the adapter's own output instead of asserting `count × gain`).

Two things to weigh. 0.55 came from settled uniform-field sample statistics of this house — roughly the median concentration these emitters produce at this grid resolution — not receptor physiology and not what moving flies experience; it sits as a constant in the shared adapter, so it applies to both levels and to any future level with different emitters or cell size. The graded curve reduces effective drive as well as adding dose dependence, so the comparison does not isolate those causes. In the final matched browser check, normal-emission banana placement produced 5 escapes versus 2 without added objects. That is useful functional evidence, not a general advantage established over binary encoding. It ships to carry concentration into the model, with its biological uncertainty disclosed.

- **Gap:** the spec required modeled, labeled sensory input; it never said whether amplitude should carry dose information or where a dose scale should live.
- **Reach:** odor tuning is now two interacting numbers (emission strength and this half-concentration). A later level with much stronger/weaker emitters or a different cell size silently gets a mis-scaled curve. The scale is a modeling choice, not a measured receptor response.
- **Provisional (in effect):** keep the graded curve with its disclosure. **Reversal:** return the amplitude closure to `1.0` for the graded pathways — a two-line revert; the new dose assertions come out with it.
- **Confidence: low-medium.** The mechanism is defensible and reversible; the placement of a house-fitted constant in shared code (rather than an authored per-level `fieldConfig` value) is what you might have decided differently.

### N4. Ship observable influence without promising reliable attraction or repulsion
**User scope decision, with provisional modeling.**

Fan-free comparisons now demonstrate that food placement changes outcomes. They do not establish that every food location helps or that vinegar consistently repels. The user prioritizes discovery through play and requested shipping once mechanics somewhat work. The interface separates real connectivity from modeled sensory input and avoids strategy-revealing object descriptions.

- **Provisional call:** retain the wired object variety, ordinary emissions, one fan, and food landing/walking. Do not fabricate neural signs or claim validated repulsion.
- **Reach:** future balancing must measure approach, residence and escape separately; more attraction need not mean more escape.
- **Confidence:** medium in useful player influence; low in any universal strategy or biological response claim.

### N5. The small house gets ten simulated minutes; the big house gets five at double pace
**Implementation decision within your delegated timing lever.**

Level 1 runs 6000 ticks — ten simulated minutes at 0.1 s per tick (`crates/sim/src/attempt.rs`) — at walking 0.12 / flight 0.24. Level 2 runs 3000 ticks, five minutes, with those speeds doubled to 0.24/0.48 (`turn-the-corner.ts`). The larger, longer route gets half the clock and compensates with faster flies; the teaching level gets the maximum allowed horizon at normal pace. Each was reached separately: house 1 needed ten minutes before exit-side placement produced any escapes; house 2 escaped nobody in five minutes until movement sped up. In watch time it is invisible — Fast divides each horizon by 60 s, so level 1 plays at 10× and level 2 at 5×, both about a minute (`packages/sim-client/src/playback.ts`).

- **Gap:** both duration and pace were delegated levers; nothing said they should be consistent across levels.
- **Reach:** cross-level escape comparisons mix two physical regimes; doubled speed also changes collision/clutter interaction in house 2.
- **Provisional (in effect):** keep both per level. **Reversal:** level-file values; changing them invalidates that level's recorded comparisons.
- **Confidence: medium.** Each number is individually justified; the asymmetry is what you might unify.

### N6. The exit is a floor-level French window
**Implementation decision, previously banked as needs-user; unchanged.**

A walking fly crosses out at floor level. The art is outward-opening full-height glass with no raised sill, because the core exit is a floor-level opening in the wall segment; a threshold would make the picture contradict the physics.

- **Gap:** the spec fixed the physical exit, not its architectural dressing.
- **Reach:** final exit art must stay a physically clear floor-level route unless the core exit changes.
- **Provisional (in effect):** keep the dressing. **Reversal:** art-only swap.
- **Confidence: medium.**

## Maintenance concern — non-blocking

### U1. Retired prototype objects still ship in the catalog, the asset registry and the bundle
**Implementation decision (a leftover, not a working choice).**

The spec says scent crumbs are "a discarded prototype lure" that does not belong in the campaign inventory, and lamp/shade cues were never established as campaign mechanics. In shipped code all three are still first-class catalog entries with sensory effects (`crates/sim/src/placement.rs`), listed in `tool_catalog()` (`placement.rs`), with GLB models wired into the placement registry (`assets/tools/registry.ts` — `crumbs.glb` 19 KB, `lamp.glb` 13 KB, `shade.glb` 11 KB), which a Vite `?url` import emits into the release bundle. Neither level lists them in inventory, so no player can place one today; the cost is not player-visible and not meaningful weight. The cost is that the release carries reachable retired content whose own spec says it was discarded, and that whoever authors the next level reads the catalog as the list of supported objects.

Same leftover, second half: the renderer resolves a placement's mesh by kind and **silently skips** a placement with no registered model (`packages/game-renderer/src/placement-models.ts`, `if (!source) continue;`), throwing only when the *catalog* entry is missing — so a half-retired object fails as an invisible-but-simulated object rather than loudly. Related dormant surface: `LevelDef.zappers`, the circular diagnostic hazard regions (`crates/sim/src/attempt.rs`, validated and swept in `crates/sim/src/body.rs`), is empty in both levels because the real zapper is a native mesh hazard — a second hazard representation ships beside the one in use.

- **Gap:** the plan scheduled a leftover audit for release (slice 17) but never stated the rule that audit enforces.
- **Reach:** what stays in the catalog is what the next level author treats as available and what future contract text must keep describing.
- **Corrected decision to redo from:** the shipped campaign catalog, asset registry and level schema expose exactly the mechanisms a shipped level can present. Diagnostics-only content is reachable through the lab fixtures, not the campaign catalog; a placement whose kind cannot be resolved to a model is an error, not an invisible object. Git history is the archive for retired prototypes — the spec's own reuse policy says so.
- **Confidence: medium-high** that you want the release surface to match the two levels; **low severity** — nothing player-visible today, so cleanup rather than a blocker.

## Sound — medium confidence: provisional tuning and reversible design you now own

Least confident first. Each is a level- or presentation-owned value that moves without touching architecture.

1. **Flies conservatively freeze rather than move through unverified geometry.** A fly walks up the steep rim of a worn shoe; the contact query can neither confirm the next small step nor prove the turn clear, so the body holds its last verified pose for the rest of that tick while time, brain and round clock advance, and unfinished work is discarded at a validated starting pose rather than accepted unchecked (`crates/sim/src/body/motion.rs`, `body/motion_consumer.rs`). One captured run held 106 blocked body-ticks. Accepting the unverified path was rejected because it means visible penetration. The cost is real: flies loiter beside clutter, and that loitering is inside today's escape numbers. The fix would be better swept resolution, not a looser tolerance.
2. **Exit-room suction shape.** Room-wide 0.06 toward a point just outside the opening, rising to 0.5 within 0.9 m, combined by taking the larger, gated on exit-room membership plus line of sight (`body.rs`). The mechanism behind N1's scoring question; as a mechanism it is what you asked for and stays inside the physical rules. Provisional balance.
3. **Fan: 3 m/s, reach 3 m, half-width 0.75 m, one per level, direction owned by the map.** Dropped in a doorway, its jet can overcome a fly's sideways wandering, with linear falloff and wall occlusion (`placement.rs`; heading canonicalized from `PlacementRules.fan_heading`, `placement.rs` — level 1 west, level 2 east). Rotation controls were removed at your direction; chained-fan solutions are excluded by the one-fan inventory. The final scent comparisons intentionally omit the fan, so physical blowing cannot masquerade as odor influence.
4. **Household and food odor strengths are game assumptions.** Radius 0.75 m throughout; emission 1.0 apple/banana/crumbs, 1.5 dirty dishes, 0.35 worn shoes, 0.2 laundry, 1.0 repellent vinegar (`placement.rs`). Sleeping cat and spider web emit nothing — context and hazard. Normal emission is used consistently; banana-only and global 3× were isolation experiments and were **not** adopted. Explicitly not measurements of real materials.
5. **Vinegar keeps its exploratory repellent wiring and its name.** You asked for the name and permitted biologically motivated wiring to stay despite inconsistent responses. Its cue drives the inhibitory-labeled population; real vinegar can attract flies, so the game calls it a strong acidic scent without claiming a measured biological concentration-to-current correspondence. A later suggestion to rebind it to a geosmin-associated pathway was declined — that evidence concerns a different chemical.
6. **Olfactory steering reads firing, not voltage.** The turn adds four times the right-minus-left *spike fraction* of the olfactory descending groups (`crates/sim/src/lif.rs`); thrust, flight thrust, flight steering and the no-olfactory fallback keep the voltage readout. The reason is mechanical: a spiking neuron resets to its floor, so the group that just fired most reads *lowest* in voltage — a voltage readout inverts the signal. The ×4 is a calibration from fixed-input response magnitudes, not a measured gain, and it costs some idle turning.
7. **Lateral detection floors: 0.05 cue units and 0.01% left/right contrast** (`sensory.rs`). At real antenna spacing (~0.2 mm) the earlier 5% and 0.1% cutoffs discarded a signal the field resolved. Equal or sub-floor senses send no current to either side. A model calibration, not a biological threshold.
8. **Twenty flies, ten walking and ten flying, in a seeded cluster.** Positions, headings and initial modes come from a dedicated stream of the root seed inside the authored spawn box (`crates/sim/src/spawn.rs`; boxes in both level files), and placement reserves that whole box, so replay and paired experiments reproduce the exact swarm. The 10/10 ratio is authored to read as a loose swarm.
9. **Timed rounds with no energy model.** Campaign flies carry the `timed` life policy — no reserve, starvation or feeding bouts; the round ends at the timer or when everyone escaped (`body.rs`). Timed records write zero into the shared reserve slot and the UI hides it, keeping one record format instead of a nullable field. Timer-expired flies keep the distinct `timedOut` outcome in diagnostics and read as "dead" to players (`apps/web/src/science-panel.tsx`, `playback.tsx`). Finite-reserve diagnostics survive behind the lifecycle lab for the deferred feeding work.
10. **Playback starts in Fast.** Fast is `max(1, authored seconds / 60)`, so a round is about a minute to watch; Real time is one click away with the same recording and cursor (`packages/sim-client/src/playback.ts`; default at `playback.tsx`). An early finish ends early rather than stretching.
11. **The MVP presentation package** (accepted as good enough; reversible, physics untouched): flies enlarged at wide zoom toward the camera’s readable screen-size target with a world-size ceiling and a 0.8 growth exponent; only foreground walls fade, back walls stay opaque; a wall shell that would hide an enlarged fly ghosts out; the yellow ring carries a thin dark outer edge so it reads on yellow fruit; escapes play an eight-second outward climb while the camera holds the exit; zapped flies get a skull billboard; wing animation runs 5× authored rate in replay while walking wings stay still. Selection starts on fly 0 as the details subject with no follow until a click, and there is no Overview button.
12. **Level layout and hazard placement.** Both houses force three interior doorway crossings and keep a wrong-turn room; house 1's final approach lies in the living room; the web sits in an out-of-the-way study corner of house 1 and the zapper in house 2's hall with room to route around it (`open-window.ts`, `turn-the-corner.ts`). Furniture is closed-base so visible floor matches collision floor; kitchens use light tile selected by room ID. The final browser campaign verifies completion; layout difficulty remains provisional.
13. **Progress and its award moment.** Best stars per level and last editable placements live in `localStorage`, unlocks derive from best stars (`apps/web/src/progress.ts`), and a failed write never kills the session. Stars are awarded when *playback* reaches the result — not when computation finishes, which leaked outcomes into the level menu — once per attempt, and rewinding keeps them. "Put objects away" clears the arrangement and preserves earned stars.
14. **Failure is presented as a game event.** If production stops, the last poses stay on screen at frozen replay time with a short interruption message and a route back to setup; the technical cause goes to the console and the diagnostic lab, never the game UI.

## Sound — high confidence: the architecture you now own

**Simulation ownership and process shape.** One Rust core (`crates/sim`) owns neural state, sensory fields, geometry, body, attempts and outcomes; a thin `wasm-bindgen` crate exposes it; one dedicated Worker runs it. Wire types are generated from Rust with `ts-rs` into `packages/sim-client`; the renderer imports data types, never the React app. No second simulation, no pathfinding controller, no server. The Worker lives across editing and playback, so retrying does not reload the graph.

**Attempt identity.** Graph hash + a build ID computed from the whole simulation source tree and toolchain (adding a module changes it) + tuning hash + level content hash + root seed + fly count + placements canonicalized and sorted by ID. Rust computes those identities; the browser never re-derives them. Resolved sources, food surfaces and wind are exposed separately from authored content, so a retry cannot resolve the same placements twice. An error mid-tick ends that attempt rather than retrying it with divergent per-fly histories.

**Transport and archive.** `start | grantCredits | cancel` in; `ready | frames | complete | error` out; every message tagged with attempt ID *and* a per-start transport generation, so restarting with a reused ID cannot accept the previous run's queued messages (`packages/sim-client/src/attempt-client.ts`). At most two unacknowledged chunks; the Worker yields between small batches and produces only when credited. Records are packed binary at schema version 4 with a 128 MiB cumulative cap computed from the actual schema before allocation, f64 voltages preserved, explicit presence flags so an absent sample is never a zero, at most 8 events per fly per tick and at most 16 named groups (`crates/sim/src/record.rs`, `packages/sim-client/src/record.ts`). Archive append takes ownership by transfer and detaches the caller's buffers, so nothing can mutate replay afterwards; overflow is an explicit error, never silent truncation.

**Crash containment.** A native trap or failed destructor retires the whole Worker and the next setup creates a fresh one; ordinary rejected input keeps the healthy Worker. When cleanup also fails, the original cause is preserved and the cleanup failure logged separately (`attempt-client.ts`).

**Setup editing.** Every edit builds a candidate list, Rust validates it, and the reply is one atomic replacement setup: placements, derived remaining inventory and resolved food triangles together. A rejected edit changes nothing, and there is no separately decremented counter to repair. Footprints fit inside one room and clear walls, the whole spawn area, reserved regions, the exit segment and one another, while sensory fields may overlap. Hover validation keeps the newest pointer position instead of queueing intermediates.

**Contact and motion.** `parry3d-f64` pinned at `=0.30.2` with `enhanced-determinism` (`crates/sim/Cargo.toml`), used as a query library rather than a second physics controller; queries run internally in millimetres because metre-scale curved-mesh departure produced a wrong blocking normal, while public coordinates stay in metres. One immutable prepared contact scene serves all bodies for an attempt. A triangle is skipped only if it cannot obstruct the requested translation (10 nm plane tolerance); a turn applies only if the swept rotation is proven clear, otherwise the exact retained quaternion is used for translation; a penetrative candidate impact retains the verified prefix; departure from a support is committed only after checking; a non-coincident landing projection is treated as blocking rather than snapped; distant food is rejected by a bounding box cached with the hull before exact queries. Support sampling returns a candidate configuration plus its witness point — never permission to move. Malformed input and genuine query failures stay errors.

**Replay fidelity.** The body's timestamped position/orientation knots are packed into the archive and sampled on the playback thread through the *same* Rust routine via the existing WASM module — no second brain, world or contact scene — so replay cannot cut a different route through a fruit. Recorded quaternions are rejected at the archive boundary if non-finite or off unit length by more than 1e-8, then interpolated along the short arc. Motion phases (last mode change, terminal tick) are rebuilt from the bounded packed history on reverse seek instead of a second decoded history.

**Neural loading and arithmetic.** The artifact stores CSR with postsynaptic rows; the loader re-orients it into one outgoing table so silent neurons cost nothing, visiting firing sources in the order each receiving neuron previously summed its inputs — same f64 results, same download format. Validated weights go straight into their final slots without an intermediate full array. On WASM only, `libm` supplies one shared argument reduction for the Box–Muller angle (`Cargo.toml`), native math unchanged, with exact output comparisons in tests. Cadence stays one neural update per 0.1 game seconds (`crates/sim/src/attempt.rs`); rendering interpolates and never drives neural updates.

**Sensory routing.** `FieldSet.sample` is the sole sampler; attractive and repellent odor are independent concentrations on one shared wall-aware transport grid with no-flux solid boundaries and doorway exchange, sharing geometry and scratch storage. Attractive odor plus the room-local exit cue drives excitatory-labeled populations, repellent odor the inhibitory-labeled ones, brightness the vision groups; each pathway may be enabled at most once per attempt with its own gain, currents add before the neural update, and duplicate entries are rejected rather than quietly doubled. Neurons read directly as motor output are excluded from stimulation, so a response must travel through the circuit. Wind is a world-space force, and the same sampled cell vectors drive body displacement and odor advection — never a hidden heading bias.

**Outcomes.** Exactly `escaped | starved | zapped | caught | timedOut` (`body.rs`); escape requires a swept outward crossing of the physical opening; terminal flies stop stepping; crossings count once; aggregation and stars live in `sim`, and the browser never infers an outcome from a disappearance. Hazard contact returns one value — none, or its kind — so a web cannot report an electrical death; hazard roles attach to the visible mesh and are independent of food eligibility, so clutter can support a fly without feeding it.

**Geometry as one source.** The core exports rooms, wall segments, doorways and solid footprints; the renderer maps those bounds to authored unit parts and never invents an obstacle. Solids block every body mode, sensory visibility, field transport and placement; presentation height does not enable overflight. Levels are TypeScript JSON literals (`apps/web/src/levels/`) and the topology test reads those same literals, so changing a doorway in the game changes what the test checks — no parallel map fixture. Furniture identity allows quarter turns only, swapping width and depth, since arbitrary angles need a different collision representation.

**Assets and materials.** One shared native dimension catalogue is read by Blender, the simulation and the browser, so an outdated export fails validation instead of being stretched; it participates in build identity. GLB convention is +Y up, +Z forward with the pivot at ground contact, bound once in a renderer transform. Local replacements validate bounds and report rather than silently correct. Edible geometry is baked from the visible GLB into the core's triangle surface with exact float JSON parsing (`serde_json` `float_roundtrip`, `Cargo.toml`) so both sides get bit-identical coordinates; identical transformed positions share one contact vertex identity across shading seams; each food identity must be one closed outward-facing boundary. Furniture resources are owned per model template, so replacing a cabinet updates every placement and frees the old mesh. Object-tray thumbnails render offline from the real assets instead of a WebGL context per button. `assets/house/palette.json` remains the shared sRGB role registry consumed by `packages/game-renderer/src/house-materials.ts`, with photographic materials layered over it rather than replacing it. Skeleton sharing for cloned flies lives in the pinned `three@0.185.1` patch (`patches/`), where cloning already belongs.

**Science panel honesty.** The brain view colours only measured soma positions by recorded *group* activity and leaves other cells neutral — group summaries never stand in for per-cell spikes, and missing positions are omitted rather than invented. Voltage and firing fraction are labeled distinctly. Group connectivity is exported from the selected graph (edge count, summed positive and summed absolute negative weight per pair); the UI may normalize widths but must not invent edges. Tooltips distinguish real connectome data from modeled dynamics, injection and decoding. Card history reads a bounded window of packed records for nearby cards only, rereading originals on seek. Olfactory readout candidates whose source annotation contradicts the "descending neuron" label fail at export with their ID rather than being swapped for better-steering cells.

**Two browser apps.** `apps/web` (game plus `/lab/*` diagnostic routes over the same owners) and `apps/asset-lab` (model replacement through the production renderer). About is served as a query on the origin root so a plain static file server needs no index fallback. The exit glow is renderer-only and adds no sensory current.

**Port fidelity guards.** The four-neuron reference fixture accepts 1e-12 float differences while requiring exact spikes and countdowns; injected noise in fixtures is already scaled and added directly; the fixture generator observes the running Python oracle's own locals rather than reimplementing its equations.
