# Five bananas in the final room

Three paired seeds, twenty flies per run, ten simulated minutes, production graph and binary sensory adapter. No fan; the existing near-door exit suction is identical across conditions. Five placements are legal in the scratch inventory; this does not change campaign inventory.

| Added objects | Unique final-room entrants by seed | Escapes by seed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| None | 8 / 8 / 8 | 5 / 5 / 4 |
| One banana at entrance | 6 / 10 / 9 | 2 / 2 / 5 |
| Five bananas in final room | 7 / 14 / 12 | 2 / 8 / 6 |

The controls are banked in [post-contact placement evidence](../../15/food-after-contact/README.md). Five bananas increased aggregate recruitment (33 versus24 arrivals) with a small aggregate escape increase (16 versus14). One seed worsened. This is exploratory paired evidence, not a reliable player success rate or proof of a biological dose response. No recorded food support occurred; physical feeding cannot explain these results.

The current adapter discards concentration after selecting a detectable side. Additional sources change the field and which sensory side activates; they do not increase per-cell current once detected. A separate sixteen-fly comparison will isolate normal/threefold emission from concentration-dependent neural encoding before changing campaign defaults.

The coverage probe samples static floor positions and headings at three field ages using the actual adapter. It describes input coverage, not observed fly trajectories. Its summed-current comparison must not be interpreted as per-cell amplitude: sensory groups can have unequal membership. Raw run results, configuration, diagnostic source and hashes preserve the reproducible conditions.
