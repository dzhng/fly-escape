# Shared lighting verification

The shared world owns daylight and ambient authoring; room details own sconces. The two campaign directions preserve their existing appearance while removing sunlight mutation from `setContactGeometry`. Physical rendering receives the same authored lights, with independently owned transforms and shadow resources. The authoring accepts room geometry and a daylight direction, never an exit or a resolved scoring setup.

The stable Y=0..1 room envelope preserves the initial presentation light/shadow calculation. Later asset-bound changes no longer move the sun. This is a deliberate determinism choice: lighting stays attached to the authored room, rather than changing after model loading or placement refresh. The default diagnostic diagonal is a fallback policy; campaign content explicitly provides its direction.

TypeScript checking, 90 renderer tests, eight web tests and two asset tests pass. Both production builds pass. Tests cover real GLB construction, shared mounted-light placement through all four rotations in both modes, physical/presentation daylight transforms and shadow projections, actual ambient and sconce presence, and independent resource disposal. The independent Codex review reported no actionable findings and independently ran typechecking and 98 renderer/web tests. Closeout afterward moved option validation ahead of allocation and replaced physical rendering's access to the old field source radius with the emitter's direct distance; focused tests and both builds passed again.

Four final production pairs cover both campaign setups at overview and the same right-button orbit. The capture uses the existing [capture script](capture-world.mjs), two independently served production builds, fresh storage, 1440×1000, DPR 1 and a frozen presentation clock. [Build identities and PNG hashes](lighting-builds.json), [browser observations](lighting-browser-check.json), and [comparison metrics](lighting-comparison.json) identify this follow-up. Every pair is byte-identical. The existing `world-reference`, `world-candidate` and `world-comparison` PNGs are reused only after verifying that all 20 new full frames, crops and differences are byte-identical to them.

A fresh screenshot critique was attempted but the global agent limit prevented it. The skill's explicit adversarial fallback was used with full frames and enlarged crops. The strongest visible objections, written before acceptance, are:

- Bright yellow exit illumination removes floor texture near Finish in both levels, so a reader could mistake the feature for an overexposed render; it is equally present in the reference.
- Translucent wall and doorway bands cross furniture, floor props and the bed in overview and orbit views, weakening depth separation; the corresponding reference has the same intersections.
- The small fruit, shoes and lower-edge spider are difficult to identify at overview scale, and enlarged crops expose stair-stepping along thin edges and labels; these are existing scale/antialiasing limits in both variants.
- The kitchen and warm walls in Turn the Corner look broadly bright with modest local contrast, but the visible furniture, tile boundaries and sconce fixtures remain present and spatially coherent in both views.

The verdict is acceptance for preserving campaign presentation, not a redesign of those existing limitations. These frames do not establish physically accurate illumination or retinal image quality. The owning slice still must verify the shared lights through the actual acquisition renderer, including its exposure, shadow sampling, occlusion and output response. ExitGlow remains presentation-only; no new photometry is assigned to movable lamp or shade assets. No attempt, full campaign or performance benchmark ran in this pass.
