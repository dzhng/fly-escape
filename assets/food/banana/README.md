# Banana shape preparation

One ordinary medium household banana is assumed: approximately 22 cm long and 4 cm thick, lying on its side. The larger cut stalk/collar and smaller recessed blossom scar distinguish orientation. Both join the curved body in one closed skin; the stalk cross-section follows its bend rather than flattening at the tip. Its closed skin is a single baked surface in native metres, with a grounded envelope-centre pivot; it has no separate overlapping stalk collider.

[The authoring source](author.py) owns dimensions and reuses the shared house export and neutral staging helpers. This is a neutral silhouette study: yellow skin, bruising and other material treatment belong to slice 22. The exported surface is prepared for future contact evaluation, not registered as runtime food. Closed topology does not establish walking, feeding, moving contact or gameplay acceptance.

Run `author.py` through Blender, then [validate.py](validate.py) to inspect the reimported GLB and reload the saved native scene. The [contact baker](../../../apps/asset-lab/scripts/export-contact.ts) writes [canonical contact geometry](contact.json), joining only exactly coincident positions as described in the food asset contract. This preserves closed skin across cap-normal splits and is tested through core containment, without registering the banana as gameplay food. The durable topology validator separately checks welded manifold edges, winding, positive volume, triangle area and nonadjacent BVH overlaps. Its 10 nm weld applies only to the temporary GLB inspection mesh; it never modifies the authored or exported asset and is not the contact export rule. Bounds remain owned by the shared exporter, while this validator checks surface integrity. It is a bounded shape check, not an exact arithmetic collision proof.

The [terminal shape evidence](../../../specs/help-the-fly-escape/assets/evidence/21/banana-terminal/README.md) records fixed-view comparison and the reverse blossom diagnostic. Material differentiation remains separate from terminal geometry.
